# Dining Readiness Index: report copy guardrails

These rules apply to every line in the quiz results, the "Explore in-depth" view, the emailed PDF report and the follow-up email. Every text block must pass them before it is approved. After that, the report only uses approved blocks.

Status: draft for Purusharth and Compass to review. 19 Nov 2026.

---

## 1. Who we are talking to

The reader is a Principal, Vice Chancellor, Dean, Trustee, administrator or dining head. They are busy and senior, and they took a 60-second quiz at an event. Write for them.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Write plainly and warmly, as a knowledgeable peer would. | Lecture, talk down, or sound like an ad. |
| Use "you" and "your institution". | Use "the client", "the user" or "the respondent". |
| Keep sentences short: 1 idea, about 20 words at most. | Write long paragraphs or stack clauses. |
| Name the people who benefit: students, parents, staff, the institution. | Use vague benefits like "stakeholders" or "the ecosystem". |

## 2. Words

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| institution, school, campus | client, account, customer |
| cafeteria, dining, kitchen | canteen (unless the guest says it), mess |
| chefs, the Chartwells team | vendor, supplier, contractor |
| nutrition education | food literacy |
| nutritionist-approved, age-appropriate | clinically proven, doctor-approved |
| future-ready, strongest level | perfect, best-in-class, world-class, gold standard |
| improve, add, set up, start | revolutionise, transform, unlock, elevate, supercharge |

Banned filler words, which read as machine-written: *delve, seamless, robust, holistic, leverage, empower, journey* (unless it means the actual "Your journey" strip), *in today's fast-paced world, it's important to note, navigate the landscape, game-changer, cutting-edge*.

## 3. Numbers and claims

- **Every statistic names its source** on the same page. For example: "Source: Nourishing India's Next Generation, a Compass Group India school food study, 2026."
- Use only statistics Compass has published or approved. Don't round a number up or reword it so it says more than the source does. The source says "health professionals", so we never write "doctors".
- **No invented outcomes.** Don't write "cuts waste by 40%" or "improves grades" unless Compass has a figure from a real site with a source.
- Score maths must always add up: area scores out of 15, a total out of 45, and the "+X to next stage" points must match the game plan.

## 4. Food safety and allergens (highest risk)

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Describe the process: "a written allergen procedure", "trained staff", "separate prep areas", "audit records". | Promise outcomes: "allergen-free", "100% safe", "zero risk", "guaranteed". |
| Say "helps reduce risk" or "makes it easier to show inspectors". | Imply legal compliance we haven't checked ("FSSAI-compliant") unless Compass confirms the wording. |
| Point to Chartwells' trained team and systems. | Suggest the institution's current kitchen is unsafe or breaking the law. |

## 5. Nutrition and health

- Talk about balanced menus, age-appropriate portions, and fresh, whole food. Don't make medical or weight-loss claims ("prevents obesity", "boosts immunity", "improves concentration").
- Use only nutrition wording the Compass nutritionist has signed off.
- Never single out a child, a body type, or a family's food choices.

## 6. Scores and tone

- **No shaming.** A low score means "a lot of room to grow", not failure. Start with what is working, then the opportunity.
- Stage names are fixed. Use exactly the names the scoring uses; don't make up new labels.
- Every "where you are today" line comes with a next step. Never point out a gap without saying how to close it.
- Don't compare the reader with named institutions or competitors.

## 7. Recommendations (game plan)

- Each step is something concrete the institution can do, written as a verb plus an object: "Publish the weekly menu with nutrition notes".
- Steps come only from the answers given. Never recommend something the guest already does at the top level.
- Chartwells' role is described as support ("how Chartwells helps"). We don't pressure the reader with lines like "act now" or "limited time".
- A full score (45 / 45) still ends with a next step: Chartwells helps the institution maintain it. Never tell a top scorer there is nothing left to do.
- Don't promise timelines or prices in the report. Next steps say "review these results with Chartwells".

## 8. Brand

- Logos: education brand (Chartwells) on the left, parent company (Compass Group) on the right. Use only official files, never stretched or recoloured.
- Write "Chartwells" (one word, no apostrophe) and "Compass Group India".
- Contact details must match the approved ones: sarika.chauhan@compass-group.co.in, +91 96671 80483.

## 9. Personal data

- The report uses only the institution name, role, name and the 9 answers.
- Never quote a seller's private notes in anything the guest sees.
- The consent line must match the approved consent text exactly. It is still a placeholder until Compass confirms it.

## 10. Format

- No em dashes ( — ). Use a comma, a full stop or a colon instead.
- Use sentence case for headings. Don't use exclamation marks.
- Use Indian English spelling (programme, colour, centre). Write numbers as numerals ("3 areas", "45").
- Nothing marked "draft", "TBC" or "lorem" may appear in anything a guest sees.

---

## 30-second checklist before approving a block

1. Would a Principal be comfortable reading this aloud to parents?
2. Does every number have a source, and is it worded no more strongly than the source?
3. Does anything promise a safety, health or academic outcome? Remove it.
4. Is there a banned word, an em dash, or "vendor"?
5. If this is a gap, does it come with a next step?
6. Is it under about 20 words a sentence?
